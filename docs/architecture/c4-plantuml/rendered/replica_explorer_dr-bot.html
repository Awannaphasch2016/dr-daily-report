GORE — Goal-Oriented Requirements view

GORE (KAOS-style) treats the dr-bot replica as a partially-satisficed goal model. The root goal — "AWS reality matches stated intent in TF source" — refines into sub-goals; obstacles are predicates whose negation is currently true; agents are the actors authorised to resolve each obstacle. Satisficing posture — what we're accepting as approximately met

GORE distinguishes strict satisfaction (goal predicate true now) from satisficing (goal accepted as approximately met given constraints). For dr-bot post-Stage-1:

  • Strict: G_audit — every disagreement is journaled before any destructive verb (Plug #2 / BS-2). Non-negotiable.
  • Satisficed: G_membership accepts a 24–48h window where 3 HALT-residue entities are un-managed (Option B). Trade: drain the deprecating state file faster, vs continuous TF management of all resources.
  • Satisficed: G_config for the 4 Doppler Lambdas is met by lifecycle.ignore_changes = [environment] rather than perfect declarative parity. Trade: TF declares the contract, runtime adds secrets — vs putting secrets into .tf.
  • Satisficed: G_config for Aurora engine_version is met by absorbing the auto-upgrade and adding lifecycle.ignore_changes = [engine_version]. Trade: AWS controls minor-version cadence, .tf is informational.

Why GORE is load-bearing for /tf-aws

"Load-bearing" in the carpentry sense: knock these words out and the skill stops working. GORE gives /tf-aws a way to mark, for both Edge A (.tf ↔ .tfstate) and Edge B (.tfstate ↔ AWS), which goals must hold strictly and which can be accepted as good-enough given real-world constraints. Strict goals: G_audit (every disagreement journaled) and the strict side of G_source (every state-bound resource declared). Good-enough-acceptable goals: parts of G_config (the 4 Doppler Lambdas — runtime injection makes perfect parity unreachable) and parts of G_membership (the HALT-residue 24–48h window during Stage-1 drain). Phase D's triage rules are that marking module — each verdict resolves one obstacle on one sub-goal.

GORE vocabulary maps 1:1 onto skill body sections: goal ↔ Tuple Effects Invariant; obstacle ↔ membership/config finding; agent ↔ verb (audit/diff/absorb/prune/sync). Take those words out and the Phases lose their backbone. Recommend: GORE is the load-bearing vocabulary for skill body.

Phenomenology — Husserlian profile view

Phenomenology treats the three columns of the Realization view as Abschattungen (profiles) of one and the same noematic object — the dr-bot system as intended. Each profile is given by a different act with its own characteristic mode of fulfillment and emptiness. This view doesn't tell you what to do; it tells you what kind of error a finding is — what category of partiality it represents. Snapshot is post-Stage-1 (2026-05-01).

What phenomenology adds that GORE doesn't

GORE flattens every finding into "obstacle on goal" — useful, but lossy. Phenomenology distinguishes kinds of partiality:

  • Empty intention ≠ passive synthesis. GORE sees both as "a goal is not satisfied"; phenomenology sees one as nothing-yet-realised on the side of the authoring-act, the other as realisation-without-an-act. Different remedies follow.
  • Modalisation is not absence. HALT-residue is not "we forgot a resource"; it is a deliberate suspension of the positing-character of P_state. The entity is given as un-managed, not given as missing. GORE collapses this distinction.
  • Thematization of horizon names what the audit does when it surfaces an ORPHAN: the horizon (everything not currently thematic) is partially brought into thematic givenness. The audit can never thematize all of P_aws; what's outside y.filter stays horizon (BS-4).
  • Reflective intentionality is the only category that bottoms out the regress: the audit looking at itself is what blocks BS-6 (audit-execution skew).

Why phenomenology is not load-bearing for skill body

GORE produces verbs; phenomenology produces nouns. It gives /tf-aws's findings a categorical vocabulary — useful for: framing essays, distinguishing kinds of partiality, explaining why "fix it" isn't always the right verb. Recommend: phenomenology lives in evolution notes, journals, and pedagogical blocks at the head of the skill — not in Phase descriptions. The skill body needs verbs.
